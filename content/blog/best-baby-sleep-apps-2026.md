## What a Sleep App Can Help With

Broken nights can make it hard to remember which nap was short or when bedtime shifted. A log can keep those notes together so you can review several days rather than rely on memory.

That is a practical reason to use a tracker, not evidence that an app will improve sleep. Planning tools may suggest changes, but parents still need to decide whether those changes fit their child and seek clinical advice when needed.

The categories below address different tasks.

## Best White Noise and Sound Apps

A [small 1990 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2405784/) studied 40 newborns aged 2–7 days: 80% in the white-noise group fell asleep within five minutes, compared with 25% in the control group. It measured falling asleep, not longer-term sleep or safety. A [2014 study of 14 infant sleep machines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24590753/) found maximum-volume outputs could exceed the 50 dBA limit recommended for hospital nurseries, even at a distance. That nursery limit is not a universal safe home volume. Keep sound low, place the device well away from the cot, keep cords out of reach and avoid prolonged loud exposure.

**Hatch Baby** is Hatch’s current baby sound-and-light product. Its [support page](https://help.hatch.co/hc/en-us/articles/34903995447959-Your-Hatch-Baby-Purchase) says basic lights, sounds and Time-for-Rise/Time-for-Bed cues remain available without the premium subscription. Guided support and membership content require checking the current package and renewal terms. The same page gives inconsistent three- and six-month inclusion periods, so confirm the included membership term and hardware price on the current product offer before purchasing. We have not tested whether the cues improve early waking.

**SNOOZ** offers several devices. Its [Original and Pro models use a real fan for sound](https://getsnooz.com/), rather than a recording. The portable Go 2 has a night light; features differ by model. A preference for fan sound is subjective, not evidence of better infant sleep.

**Calm** and **Headspace** offer general sleep audio rather than infant schedule planning. Calm describes [Sleep Stories, music and meditations](https://support.calm.com/hc/en-us/articles/360016390734-Using-Calm-s-Content-to-Fall-Asleep); Headspace lists [sleepcasts, music and wind-down content](https://www.headspace.com/sleep). We have not verified suitability for babies or toddlers and do not recommend them on that basis.

## What to Look for Before You Download

Before downloading or paying, check a few concrete details.

Check the age range, what is included in the free tier, and whether the quoted subscription price is billed monthly or annually. If several caregivers will log entries, check the sharing arrangements. Review what health or family data the provider collects, how to export it and how to cancel. Be cautious of promises of overnight results; an advertised feature is not a guarantee of improvement.

## When an App Isn't Enough

If your baby snores regularly, gasps or has pauses in breathing during sleep, talk to your paediatrician. The [AAP’s childhood sleep apnoea guideline](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22926173/) recommends screening for snoring. If your child is currently struggling to breathe or cannot be woken, seek emergency medical help rather than using an app.

Similarly, if your own sleep deprivation is affecting your mental health — persistent anxiety, irritability, difficulty concentrating during the day — that is worth discussing with your doctor, not just managing through an app.

## The Bottom Line

Choose the tool that matches the task and that your family can maintain. Logging, a draft timetable, background sound and a course serve different purposes; none guarantees that a child will sleep better.

You do not need to optimise everything at once. Pick the area where you need the most help right now, try one app for a week, and build from there.
